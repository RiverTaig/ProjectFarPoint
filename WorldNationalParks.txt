Here are **40 national parks around the world** that feel, in different ways, like the “greatest hits” of U.S. parks: big scenery, strong trail systems, wildlife, geology, and memorable day hikes. I leaned toward parks where you can base yourself nearby and do excellent day hikes without needing a full expedition. For context, places like Banff have more than 1,600 km of maintained trails, New Zealand’s Great Walks system highlights premier national-park tracks, the Dolomites are a UNESCO-listed mountain landscape, and Table Mountain is an unusually accessible urban-wilderness park. ([Parks Canada][1])

## Canada

**1. Banff National Park, Canada**
Banff is probably the closest non-U.S. equivalent to a blend of Glacier, Rocky Mountain, and Yosemite. Day hikes range from easy lake walks at Louise and Moraine to big alpine routes like Sentinel Pass, Cory Pass, and Plain of Six Glaciers. It is spectacular, popular, and very well developed for hikers.

**2. Jasper National Park, Canada**
Jasper feels like Banff’s wilder, roomier cousin, with huge valleys, glaciers, wildlife, and fewer crowds. Day hikes like Wilcox Pass, Bald Hills, Valley of the Five Lakes, and Sulphur Skyline give you major Rocky Mountain payoffs. It is a great choice if you like Glacier or Grand Teton but want a slightly more spacious feel.

**3. Yoho National Park, Canada**
Yoho is small but ridiculously scenic, with waterfalls, emerald lakes, cliffs, and high alpine basins. Iceline Trail is one of Canada’s great day hikes, with glacier views and big open terrain. It feels like a concentrated mix of Yosemite granite drama and Glacier-style alpine hiking.

**4. Kootenay National Park, Canada**
Kootenay is less famous than Banff but has wonderful day hikes, hot springs, canyons, burnt forests, and colorful mineral springs. Stanley Glacier and Floe Lake approaches give you classic Canadian Rockies scenery. It is a good “avoid the worst crowds” alternative while still being near Banff.

**5. Gros Morne National Park, Canada**
Gros Morne has fjords, coastal cliffs, tundra-like highlands, and exposed mantle rock at the Tablelands. Day hikes include Gros Morne Mountain, Green Gardens, and the Tablelands trails. It feels unlike most U.S. parks, but the drama is on the level of Acadia plus Olympic plus a little Iceland.

**6. Pacific Rim National Park Reserve, Canada**
Pacific Rim is a coastal park of rainforest, beaches, tidepools, and stormy Pacific scenery on Vancouver Island. Day hikes are shorter but atmospheric, especially rainforest boardwalks, beach walks, and coastal headland trails. Think Olympic National Park’s coastal side, but with a distinctly Canadian West Coast feel.

## Australia

**7. Blue Mountains National Park, Australia**
The Blue Mountains are one of the best day-hiking parks in Australia, with sandstone cliffs, waterfalls, eucalyptus forests, and deep valleys. Trails around Wentworth Falls, the Grand Canyon Walk, and the National Pass area feel like a mix of Zion, the Grand Canyon rim, and the Columbia River Gorge. It is also easy to reach from Sydney.

**8. Kosciuszko National Park, Australia**
Kosciuszko is Australia’s alpine national park, with broad high-country walking, wildflowers, snow gums, and the country’s highest summit. The Mount Kosciuszko summit walk is a very accessible high-point day hike. It is gentler than the Rockies but has a big open alpine feeling.

**9. Grampians / Gariwerd National Park, Australia**
Gariwerd has rugged sandstone ranges, Aboriginal rock art, waterfalls, wildlife, and excellent viewpoint hikes. Trails to the Pinnacle, Mount Rosea, and Hollow Mountain are classic day hikes. It has a bit of Zion’s sandstone feel, but with kangaroos, cockatoos, and Australian bushland.

**10. Cradle Mountain–Lake St Clair National Park, Australia**
This Tasmanian park is famous for the Overland Track, but its day hikes are outstanding too. Cradle Mountain summit, Marion’s Lookout, Dove Lake Circuit, and short rainforest walks all deliver big scenery. It feels like a southern-hemisphere cousin of Glacier or Mount Rainier, but moodier and more ancient.

**11. Freycinet National Park, Australia**
Freycinet is known for pink granite mountains, white beaches, turquoise bays, and coastal heath. The Wineglass Bay lookout and Mount Amos are superb day hikes, with Mount Amos giving a surprisingly rugged scramble for a modest elevation. It feels like Acadia or coastal California, but brighter, drier, and more exotic.

## New Zealand

**12. Fiordland National Park, New Zealand**
Fiordland is one of the world’s great mountain-and-water parks, with fjords, rainforest, waterfalls, and huge glaciated valleys. Even if the famous Milford, Kepler, and Routeburn tracks are multi-day routes, sections make exceptional day hikes. It is comparable to a wetter, steeper, more remote blend of Olympic, Glacier, and Yosemite.

**13. Mount Aspiring National Park, New Zealand**
Mount Aspiring is a South Island alpine park of glaciers, braided rivers, beech forests, and sharp peaks. Day hikes near Wanaka and Glenorchy include Rob Roy Glacier, Diamond Lake/Rocky Mountain, and parts of the Routeburn. It feels like a less crowded, more rugged version of the best U.S. mountain parks.

**14. Aoraki / Mount Cook National Park, New Zealand**
This is New Zealand’s big-peak park, centered on Aoraki/Mount Cook and surrounded by glaciers, icefalls, and turquoise lakes. Hooker Valley Track may be one of the world’s best easy-to-moderate day hikes. The scenery has a Grand Teton or Rainier-level punch, but with a uniquely Southern Alps character.

**15. Tongariro National Park, New Zealand**
Tongariro is a volcanic park with craters, lava fields, steam vents, and bright alpine lakes. The Tongariro Alpine Crossing is often described as one of the world’s great day hikes and is the obvious centerpiece. It is the best non-U.S. analogue I know for a blend of Yellowstone, Lassen, and Haleakalā.

**16. Abel Tasman National Park, New Zealand**
Abel Tasman is all golden beaches, clear water, forested headlands, and coastal walking. The Abel Tasman Coast Track can be broken into excellent day hikes using water taxis. It feels like a warmer, more intimate version of coastal Olympic or Channel Islands.

## South America

**17. Torres del Paine National Park, Chile**
Torres del Paine is one of the most spectacular mountain parks on Earth, with granite towers, glaciers, lakes, and wind-scoured grasslands. The Base Torres day hike is a world-class objective, while shorter hikes near Grey, Pehoé, and Mirador Condor are also excellent. It is the closest global peer to Glacier or Grand Teton in raw scenic drama, though the weather can be much harsher. ([Condé Nast Traveler][2])

**18. Los Glaciares National Park, Argentina**
Los Glaciares includes the Fitz Roy and Cerro Torre region near El Chaltén, plus the famous Perito Moreno Glacier farther south. Day hikes to Laguna de los Tres, Laguna Torre, and Loma del Pliegue Tumbado are among the best in Patagonia. It feels like a supercharged version of the Tetons with sharper peaks and bigger glaciers.

**19. Nahuel Huapi National Park, Argentina**
Nahuel Huapi surrounds Bariloche with lakes, forests, volcanic peaks, and granite ridges. Day hikes like Cerro Llao Llao, Refugio Frey, and Cerro Campanario offer huge lake-and-mountain views. It has a Lake Tahoe-meets-Glacier feel, with a more European alpine-town atmosphere.

**20. Huascarán National Park, Peru**
Huascarán protects part of the Cordillera Blanca, one of the highest and most spectacular tropical mountain ranges in the world. Laguna 69 is the famous day hike, but there are many other turquoise-lake and glacier-view routes. It is like the High Sierra or Rocky Mountain National Park lifted to Himalayan-scale elevations.

**21. Chapada Diamantina National Park, Brazil**
Chapada Diamantina is a plateau park of waterfalls, caves, mesas, swimming holes, and canyon overlooks. Day hikes to Morro do Pai Inácio, Cachoeira da Fumaça, and Vale do Pati viewpoints are the highlights. It is less like the Rockies and more like a tropical blend of Zion, Canyonlands, and the Ozarks.

**22. Tayrona National Natural Park, Colombia**
Tayrona combines Caribbean beaches, boulder-strewn coves, dry forest, rainforest, and views toward the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta. The main day hikes link beaches like Cabo San Juan with forest trails and coastal viewpoints. It feels like a tropical cousin of Virgin Islands National Park or coastal sections of Olympic.

## Central America

**23. Manuel Antonio National Park, Costa Rica**
Manuel Antonio is small, popular, and exceptionally rich in wildlife, with rainforest trails leading to beaches and viewpoints. Day hiking is not long or strenuous, but the density of monkeys, sloths, birds, and coastal scenery is outstanding. Think of it less as a Yosemite-scale park and more as a tropical wildlife-and-beach version of a U.S. park experience.

**24. Corcovado National Park, Costa Rica**
Corcovado is one of Central America’s great wilderness parks, protecting rainforest, beaches, rivers, and extraordinary biodiversity. Day hikes are usually done with guides from access points like Sirena, San Pedrillo, or La Leona. It is more remote than most U.S. parks, but in wildlife terms it is closer to a tropical Yellowstone.

**25. Arenal Volcano National Park, Costa Rica**
Arenal offers volcano views, lava-flow trails, rainforest, hanging bridges nearby, hot springs, and waterfalls in the broader area. Day hikes are moderate rather than epic, but the combination of volcano and jungle is excellent. It is a good Central American analogue to Lassen or Hawaiʻi Volcanoes, with easier tourist infrastructure.

**26. Volcán Barú National Park, Panama**
Volcán Barú protects Panama’s highest mountain, and on clear days hikers may see both the Pacific and Caribbean from the summit area. The summit route is a long, demanding day hike or overnight climb, while lower trails around Boquete are more accessible. It has the appeal of a high-volcano U.S. park, but with cloud forest and tropical birdlife.

## Europe

**27. Dolomiti Bellunesi National Park / Dolomites region, Italy**
The Dolomites are one of the world’s most beautiful mountain landscapes, with pale limestone towers, green valleys, alpine huts, and superb trails. Strictly speaking, the famous Dolomites hiking region includes multiple parks and protected areas, not just one U.S.-style national park. For day hiking, Tre Cime, Seceda, Cinque Torri, and Lago di Sorapis are as iconic as anything in the American West. ([UNESCO World Heritage Centre][3])

**28. Gran Paradiso National Park, Italy**
Gran Paradiso is Italy’s oldest national park and feels like a classic Alps wilderness of glaciers, ibex, meadows, and stone villages. Day hikes from Cogne, Valsavarenche, and Valnontey give big alpine scenery without needing technical climbing. It has a Mount Rainier or Rocky Mountain feeling, but with old European valleys and wildlife-rich meadows.

**29. Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park, Spain**
Ordesa is a Pyrenean park of deep canyons, waterfalls, cliffs, forests, and high limestone summits. The classic Ordesa Valley hike to Cola de Caballo is one of Europe’s great day walks. It feels like Yosemite Valley crossed with Zion, but greener and more alpine.

**30. Picos de Europa National Park, Spain**
Picos de Europa has jagged limestone peaks, deep gorges, pastoral valleys, and dramatic cable-car access. Day hikes around Fuente Dé, the Cares Gorge, and the Lakes of Covadonga are outstanding. It feels like a compact European version of Glacier or the Tetons, with villages and cheese instead of lodges and ranger stations.

**31. Plitvice Lakes National Park, Croatia**
Plitvice is famous for boardwalks through turquoise lakes, waterfalls, travertine barriers, and forest. The day hiking is easy to moderate, but visually unforgettable. It is not a wilderness park like Glacier, but it has the “must-see natural wonder” quality of Yellowstone’s geyser basins or Yosemite Valley.

**32. Jotunheimen National Park, Norway**
Jotunheimen is Norway’s high mountain park, with glaciers, lakes, ridges, and some of Scandinavia’s highest peaks. Besseggen Ridge is a classic day hike with a knife-edge view between blue and green lakes. It feels like a colder, more austere cousin of Glacier or Rocky Mountain National Park.

**33. Vatnajökull National Park, Iceland**
Vatnajökull is enormous and includes glaciers, volcanoes, black sands, waterfalls, and outlet-glacier landscapes. Day hikes around Skaftafell, such as the route to Svartifoss or glacier viewpoints, are very rewarding. It feels like a mix of Alaska, Hawaiʻi Volcanoes, and the more elemental parts of Yellowstone.

## Africa

**34. Table Mountain National Park, South Africa**
Table Mountain is one of the world’s great urban national parks, stretching from Cape Town’s mountain front to Cape Point. Day hikes include Platteklip Gorge, India Venster, Skeleton Gorge, Lion’s Head nearby, and Cape Point trails. It is like having a mini-Zion, coastal Acadia, and a botanical reserve attached to a major city. ([SANParks][4])

**35. Drakensberg / uKhahlamba-Drakensberg Park, South Africa**
The Drakensberg has basalt escarpments, waterfalls, grasslands, caves, and dramatic amphitheater walls. Day hikes to Tugela Gorge, Rainbow Gorge, Cathedral Peak approaches, and Giants Castle viewpoints are excellent. It has a Grand Canyon rim-meets-Glacier feel, but with African highland scenery and San rock art.

**36. Kilimanjaro National Park, Tanzania**
Kilimanjaro is best known for the multi-day summit climb, but the lower forest and moorland zones also offer memorable day hikes. Shorter hikes around Marangu, Materuni, and lower park routes give views of the mountain and lush volcanic terrain. It is not a casual U.S.-style trail park, but as a single iconic mountain it rivals Rainier or Denali in presence.

**37. Simien Mountains National Park, Ethiopia**
The Simiens are a highland park of cliffs, escarpments, gelada monkeys, lammergeiers, and immense views. Day hikes from areas like Sankaber and Geech can be arranged with local support. It feels like a wild blend of Grand Canyon, the Rockies, and an African wildlife park.

**38. Mount Kenya National Park, Kenya**
Mount Kenya has glaciers, moorlands, tarns, giant lobelias, and sharp volcanic peaks. Many people do multi-day routes, but lower-elevation day hikes around the park gates and forest zones are rewarding. It has the big extinct-volcano quality of Rainier or Kilimanjaro, with unique equatorial alpine ecology.

## Asia

**39. Chubu Sangaku National Park, Japan**
This is the heart of the Japanese Alps, with Kamikōchi as one of the most accessible and beautiful hiking gateways. Easy valley walks follow clear rivers beneath huge peaks, while harder day hikes climb toward ridges and huts. It feels like a Japanese version of Yosemite Valley or Glacier’s Many Glacier area, with excellent infrastructure and mountain culture. ([Kamikochi Official Website][5])

**40. Sagarmatha National Park, Nepal**
Sagarmatha protects the Everest region, so its scenery is obviously world-class: Himalayan peaks, Sherpa villages, monasteries, glaciers, and high valleys. Most visits are multi-day treks, but from Namche Bazaar there are superb day hikes to viewpoints, villages, and acclimatization ridges. It is less like a U.S. national park trip logistically, but in mountain grandeur it is almost in a category of its own.
